Does optimization beat 1/N?

Pick ETFs and settings, then run a walk-forward backtest of five construction methods against equal weight. Everything is computed in your browser. Every result comes with the statistics needed to tell skill from luck.

Educational project, not investment advice. Backtests describe the past under simplifying assumptions and do not predict future returns.

How it works

  • Walk-forward. On each rebalance date, a strategy sees only the trailing lookback window. New weights apply from the next trading day, and between rebalances weights drift with prices.
  • Strategies. Min variance and max Sharpe use a Ledoit-Wolf shrunk covariance matrix. Max Sharpe uses raw trailing mean returns on purpose, to show estimation error. Risk parity equalizes risk contributions. HRP (hierarchical risk parity) clusters assets by correlation. All are long-only and capped at the max weight.
  • Costs. Cost = bps × turnover, where turnover is the sum of absolute weight changes at each rebalance.
  • Sharpe. Computed on daily excess returns over 3-month T-bills (^IRX), annualized.
  • Same-risk comparison. Each strategy is levered or delevered to equal weight's volatility, borrowing at the T-bill rate. It uses full-sample volatility, so it is a comparison aid, not a tradable strategy.
  • Data. Adjusted closes from Yahoo Finance via yfinance, refreshed nightly by GitHub Actions and published only if the quality checks pass.
