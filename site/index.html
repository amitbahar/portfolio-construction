Does optimization beat 1/N?

Pick ETFs and settings, then run a walk-forward backtest of five construction methods against equal weight. Everything is computed in your browser. Every result comes with the statistics needed to tell skill from luck.

Educational project, not investment advice. Backtests describe the past under simplifying assumptions and do not predict future returns.

How it works

  • Walk-forward. On each rebalance date, a strategy sees only the trailing lookback window. New weights apply from the next trading day, and between rebalances weights drift with prices.
  • Strategies. Min variance and max Sharpe use a Ledoit-Wolf shrunk covariance matrix. Max Sharpe uses raw trailing mean returns on purpose, to show estimation error. Risk parity equalizes risk contributions. HRP (hierarchical risk parity) clusters assets by correlation. All are long-only and capped at the max weight. When the chosen funds trade on different exchanges, covariance is estimated from weekly returns, because Tel Aviv closes before New York and daily data understates how closely the markets move together.
  • Costs. Cost = bps × turnover, where turnover is the sum of absolute weight changes at each rebalance.
  • No-trade band. When set, a rebalance is skipped if every drifted weight is within the band of its new target. Otherwise the portfolio trades fully to the target. The band applies to every strategy and benchmark alike, so it cuts costs without favouring one method.
  • Lookback check. After each run, the selected strategies are run again with the other lookback windows (126, 252 and 504 days) and compared over the period in which all of them have a full window.
  • Sharpe. Computed on daily excess returns over 3-month T-bills (FRED series DTB3), annualized.
  • Same-risk comparison. Each strategy is levered or delevered to equal weight's volatility, borrowing at the T-bill rate. It uses full-sample volatility, so it is a comparison aid, not a tradable strategy.
  • Data. ETF prices are adjusted closes from Yahoo Finance, retrieved with the open-source yfinance library. The T-bill rate is from FRED (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis). Tel Aviv-listed funds are converted to US dollars at the Bank of Israel's representative exchange rate. The data is refreshed nightly by GitHub Actions and published only if the quality checks pass.
